# Happy Makers — V4 character voice bible

Status: applied in `content/book1_v4_overrides.yml`: six introductory cards, thirty case chats, six interludes and the staged finale. This is a writing contract and source review, not a certificate of rendered readability.

## Team and reader canon

**Five field detectives:** Mimi, Luli, Dilo, Alio, Nini. **Archive mentor:** Grandma Bibi. The Academy has six field-badge hooks; the reader trains for the vacant sixth field slot. Bibi never occupies that slot. “Six Happy Makers characters” may describe the illustration cast; it must never become “six field detectives plus the reader.”

The reader first meets the team, sees its work and the empty hook, receives the one black-envelope invitation, accepts on one Field Detective ID, then investigates Case 01. “Detective Six” is the offered role from the beginning. The finale earns official certification rather than revealing an identity the letter already named.

The characters are friends who enjoy doing useful things together. Their disagreements have practical consequences: a claim gets checked, a witness gets time to explain, an observation changes the next action. Avoid inventing family relationships, exceptional abilities or fixed autobiographies to fill space. An exchange must sound like people who know one another, not six tutors taking turns to repeat the objective.

## Mimi — field detective / case captain

Mimi turns a muddle into the next useful step. She notices which record needs keeping, which question should come first and who needs a manageable job. Clear actions and occasional mock-official language are her default rhythm. She is capable, warm and decisive; a personally untidy phone or bag does not make her an incompetent investigator.

Her friction is with elaborate solutions to simple problems. She enjoys Alio's enthusiasm and Dilo's inventions while asking them to finish the actual enquiry. Nini can tease her comfortably; Mimi answers rather than punishing the interruption. She gives the reader responsibility in stages, ending by bringing the original badge rack to the final check.

Applied voice: “Next trip: readable name tags.” Alio proposes satellites; Mimi replies, “My tags work indoors.” In the academy introduction she claims everything has a place, Nini finds her phone in the snack basket, and Mimi calls it a temporary place. Case 11 develops that shared joke with the phone and oat bar. Do not repeat lost-phone jokes in every later case.

Never: a schoolroom scold, threats, competence erased for comedy, generic praise after every answer, or treating the reader as a passive assistant after Case 26.

## Luli — field detective / clue analyst

Luli reads small print and asks what a statement actually proves. Her replies are often brief, but she is curious rather than contemptuous. Dry literal questions puncture inflated claims. She corrects an inference, not someone's worth. Give her room to enjoy a successful test and acknowledge another person's observation.

Her familiar friction is with Dilo's confidence and Alio's preferred adventure. Her natural partnership with Nini protects the difference between a mistaken witness and a dishonest one. By Case 26 she points to the reader's preserved maps and credits Nini's question; she does not take the discovery over.

Applied voice: Case 03, “Inspector Glitter needs a connection to the case.” Case 19, after Dilo chooses the scanner record over a digital ghost: “I like this plot development.” Case 29: “The placements belong to our detective.”

Never: “obviously,” sneering, a solution hidden in sarcasm, being right about everything, or reducing every exchange to a one-word rejection.

## Dilo — field detective / code and tech

Dilo likes competition, machines, games and an impressive name for an ordinary task. Confidence can arrive before his evidence, but he is willing to test, revise and help. His useful skill is connecting numbers and mechanisms. A boast must remain recognizably a boast; made-up facts never become authoritative puzzle information.

Luli prompts the test, Mimi keeps priorities clear, and Nini makes him listen. His arc moves from challenging the reader to beat his locker to asking for careful steps on the largest map. Leave him several straightforward competent lines so that the joke does not become his entire personality.

Applied voice: Case 05, the locker “has been briefed” about the competition. Case 18, his watch accepts correction with dignity while he makes the adjustment quietly. Case 19, he chooses the scanner record. Case 29, “I am saving the victory noise until we have a verdict.”

Never: magic hacking, invented surveillance, mocking a child's pace, reckless dangerous conduct for a punchline, or a different grand statistical claim in every file. The final archive release flap is explicitly harmless. He opens it only after Book 1 is resolved and certification completed.

## Alio — field detective / routes and reconnaissance

Alio asks the extra question, packs the helmet and imagines the expedition. His physical observations become valuable when he pauses. He uses action words, concrete equipment and enthusiastic proposals. Curiosity is his strength; a tunnel is an idea to test, not his permanent explanation for everything.

Mimi sets sequence, Luli requests evidence and Bibi gives him precise ways to help with the old plan. The route case exposes why a straight line may not be a permitted path. Later, the mud case lets him voluntarily pause a dramatic theory. The final map receives a single helmet and moral support, leaving the inference to the reader.

Applied voice: the early trophy rescue kit includes an emergency biscuit for morale; the rope is negotiable. His railway helmet is an ordinary helmet with a versatile job description. In Case 27 he negotiates one heroic finger pointing at a landmark.

Never: unsafe instructions, mockery of caution, being the fool in every exchange, repetitive tunnel explanations, or a helmet used as a substitute for an actual observation.

## Nini — field detective / people and courage

Nini gives people time to speak and notices what a louder theory misses. She can become fond of a charming idea or give an object an affectionate name, then accept the relevant distinction. Concrete questions, gentle literal replies and small sharp observations carry her humor. She is not a babyish mascot or a motivational narrator.

She can tease Mimi, make Dilo listen and work with Luli to avoid unfair blame. Case 14 checks a witness's memory without awarding “villain badges.” Case 26 develops her habit of noticing what is absent: she asks about an empty room, while the reader performs the fourteen-map test.

Applied voice: Inspector Glitter does not get promoted merely for shining; a spoon is disappointing as a dragon but excellent for soup; a worried pear belongs on the art wall, not the witness list. These are different observations, not one repeated catchphrase.

Never: baby talk, diagnosing a witness, empty reassurance, telling the reader how they must feel, or giving away CHECK THE OLD MAP before the reader derives it.

## Grandma Bibi — archive mentor / old Academy guide

Bibi is adventurous, precise about uncertainty and capable of stopping nonsense without a lecture. She remembers an object or an event more readily than its exact date. Her past supplies records to verify, not unexplained authority. A recollection with a gap is said plainly. The dated routing log, not a convenient flash of memory, proves the system's causal role.

She knows the old Academy and her childhood squad. That historical squad is separate from the five current field detectives. Her card and every present-day badge scene identify her as archive mentor. She never “becomes the sixth detective” and never holds the reader's badge slot.

Applied voice: Case 09, Dilo mistakes “I know that mark” for a complete explanation; Bibi answers that it was the complete sentence. Case 16 allows one affectionate hat joke, with Bibi's competence intact. Case 27 asks for clean hands and no heroic folding. After certification she requests one quiet minute; the Book 2 file then interrupts it.

Never: ageist comedy, a helpless-grandmother stereotype, an oracle's riddles, withholding a known essential answer to test the reader, or solving the final lock herself.

## Chat and evidence contract

All thirty cases have **3–5 short lines** under **HAPPY MAKERS CHAT**: 110 case-chat lines in the applied overlay. Chats add personality and relationships. Every case is explicitly marked `chat_is_evidence: false`; puzzle-critical constraints remain on the evidence page or Witness Board. Do not silently remove a clue from the board because a character mentions it.

Speaker line counts are Mimi 21, Luli 20, Dilo 23, Alio 20, Nini 18 and Bibi 8. These are case chats only, excluding the introduction, signals, interludes and finale. Bibi's smaller field-case presence is intentional; her decisive archive scenes matter more than equal airtime.

The same speaker order may recur at a distance when the relationship warrants it. No two consecutive case chats use the same sequence. A joke must not be repeated verbatim to make a callback recognizable. Compare the situation and changed response, not just shared words. The repetition audit measures text similarity and separately lists recurring speaker patterns.

The reader supplies verdicts, preserves placements, reconstructs the note, tests the fourteen maps, restores Rule Zero and completes the final fields. The cast may suggest a useful test; they cannot pre-solve it in the preceding chat. Signals and recaps occur after the relevant case. Final certification recognizes those specific acts of investigation.

Rendered review still required: six distinct large portrait cards; Bibi's mentor label; reliable speaker/avatar pairing; 11–12 pt readable chat; no isolated last line, clipping or accidental order change; visible distinction between chat, evidence and post-case signal; no early answer leakage. Counts alone do not prove an enjoyable book.
